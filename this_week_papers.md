#### **Title:
          The Disordered Universe 2025: Features in a $Λ>0$ Universe**
 - **Authors:** Samuel Brian, Yakov Landau, Tommaso Marini, Daniel McLoughlin, Polina Petriakova, Rajat Sharma, Dionysios Anninos, Paolo Benincasa, Guilherme Leite Pimentel
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/](https://arxiv.org/abs/)
 - **Abstract**
 A complete understanding of cosmology, from its earliest epoch all the way to the current dark energy dominated era, requires the development of a mathematical framework that incorporates quantum features in an expanding universe. Such a framework, alongside a description of the underlying degrees of freedom, is so far lacking. Several of the pertinent challenges and puzzles already arise upon considering even the most idealised model of an expanding universe --- the de Sitter spacetime and fluctuations thereof. In these lectures, we review some elementary ideas of the de Sitter physics, passing through the representation theory of the de Sitter group, aspects of canonical quantum gravity, all the way to attempts in describing quasi-local features. Throughout, we highlight open problems that motivate ongoing efforts to understand quantum gravity and cosmology in universes with $\Lambda>0$. These notes are based on a series of lectures held during the La Ricotta Summer School: The Disordered Universe 2025 from 21 July to 1 August 2025.

#### **Title:
          Minkowski metric signature as a statistical prediction of piecewise-flat quantum gravity**
 - **Authors:** Mihailo Djordjevic, Igor Prlina, Marko Vojinovic
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/](https://arxiv.org/abs/)
 - **Abstract**
 We study an ab initio calculation of the signature of the spacetime metric, within the framework of piecewise-flat models of quantum gravity. Applying numerical techniques, we evaluate the probability that any 4-simplex in a triangulation of a spacetime manifold has a given metric signature. For this we use a toy-model of quantum gravity based on quantum Regge calculus, where we ignore any effects due to dynamics and focus only on the kinematics of the configuration space. Our results may hold even for other QG models, as long as they behave similarly in the same approximation. We obtain that for a suitably chosen spacelike domain of edge lengths and the UV cutoff, the probability of observing Minkowski signature reaches approximately 85%. In contrast, allowing both spacelike and timelike edge lengths leads to the split signature being dominant, with probability between 72% and 81%. The results are essentially independent of the choice of topology and triangulation of the spacetime manifold. Our findings demonstrate that the signature of the metric does not necessarily need to be postulated in a given model of quantum gravity, but can instead be predicted from it, and we estimate the relative size of the UV cutoff and the domain for edge lengths that a given model should incorporate in order to predict the Minkowski signature with high probability. There is a potential to further increase this probability by taking into account the dynamics of the theory.
